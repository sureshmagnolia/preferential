Institutional Single Transferable Vote (STV) Framework

The Single Transferable Vote (STV) is a proportional preferential electoral system utilized internationally for university senates, municipal councils, and parliamentary assemblies. It empowers electors to rank multiple candidates in order of preference (1, 2, 3...) ensuring that no vote is wasted.

1. The Droop Quota

The minimum vote threshold required to guarantee election without exceeding the available seats:

Quota = ⌊ Total Valid Votes Vacancies + 1 ⌋ + 1
Worked Example: For 100 valid votes and 3 vacancies:
Quota = ⌊100 / (3 + 1)⌋ + 1 = 25 + 1 = 26 votes.

2. Fractional Surplus Transfers

Surplus votes above quota are transferred to voters' 2nd preferences using the Inclusive Gregory Method:

Transfer Factor = Total Votes − Quota Total Votes Received
Worked Example: If Candidate A secures 36 votes (Quota = 26):
Surplus = 36 − 26 = 10 votes.
Transfer Value = 10 / 36 = 0.2778 per ballot.

3. Lowest Candidate Elimination

When no hopeful candidate reaches the winning quota, the candidate with the fewest votes is eliminated:

Lowest Polling Candidate → ELIMINATED
Redistribution: All ballots belonging to the eliminated candidate are transferred at their full current weight to each voter's next hopeful preference.

Audit Trail & Verification Integrity

Every ballot logged into this system is permanently assigned a distinct Serial Number (e.g. BAL-0042). Scrutineers, candidates, and returning officers can inspect any individual ballot slip in the registry, verify the physical paper preferences entered, and audit how those votes transferred during the count.
